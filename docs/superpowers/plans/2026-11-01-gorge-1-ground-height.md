# The Gorge, part 1: the Ground-Height Engine Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Give every area a walkable ground height `ground.y(x, z)`, 0 on every map that exists today, and make every system that stands on the ground read it. Edges stop the cat. Slopes tilt the body and slow the climb. The camera stays out of rock and under ceilings. All of it is proven on a test-only height fixture, and nothing on any shipped map moves by a single bit.

**Architecture:**
- One new pure module, `src/ground.js`. An area may return `ground: createGround({ strips, discs, ceilings })` from its `build()`. Every other area gets the frozen `FLAT_GROUND` through `groundOf(areaData)`.
- `walk.js` resolves it once per walk as `session.ground` and hands it to each system's factory, the same way `areaData` is handed round today.
- Authored heights (perches, golden mice, knockables, collectibles, rests, camp rocks) become "metres above the ground at their own x, z". The numbers themselves don't change.
- `player.perchY` keeps meaning "metres above the ground under the cat", so `perchY === 0` still means "on the ground" everywhere.
- Each new behaviour (edge slide, uphill pace, tilt, camera rock pull) sits behind `ground.flat`. Shipped maps run exactly today's code path.

**Tech Stack:** Vanilla JS (ES modules), Three.js 0.185, Vite, Vitest.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-01-gorge-design.md`. This plan covers "Height: the new engine piece", "Builder-check rulings" and "How it is split" item 1, plus the Constraints. Also `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-editions-and-territories-design.md` §3, builder notes: "No terrain. Colliders are circles at y = 0, and ground is flat … The Gorge waits until the game can do height". This plan is that step.

## Rulings (the planner's calls, 2026-11-01; each with what it costs if wrong)

- **R1. How height is represented: shapes, not a heightmap.**
  - `createGround({ base = 0, strips = [], discs = [], ceilings = [] })`.
  - A **strip** is `{ x0, z0, x1, z1, width, y0, y1 = y0 }`: a centreline segment with a half-width each side. Height runs linearly from `y0` at `(x0,z0)` to `y1` at `(x1,z1)`, so `y0 === y1` is a flat ledge. A switchback path is a chain of strips.
  - A **disc** is `{ x, z, r, y }`: a flat landing, plateau or clifftop.
  - `ground.y(x, z)` is the **max** over every shape that contains the point, else `base`. The max rule means a ramp's top end can overlap a landing at the same height with no seam, and no authoring order matters.
  - A **ceiling** is a strip-shaped `{ x0, z0, x1, z1, width, y }`. `ground.ceil(x, z)` is the min over the ceilings that contain the point, else `Infinity`.
  - Cost if wrong: plan 2 can't draw its gorge from strips and discs and wants a polygon kind. That is one more shape in `src/ground.js`, and no caller changes.
- **R2. Edges come from the heights; there is no separate wall list.**
  - `ground.canStep(ax, az, bx, bz)` walks the segment in sub-steps of at most `SUBSTEP = 0.25` m. Each sub-step is legal when `|Δy| ≤ STEP_MAX + MAX_GRADE · len`, with `STEP_MAX = 0.12` and `MAX_GRADE = 0.5`.
  - Any rim with a drop of 0.3 m or more is therefore a low invisible wall in both directions. A 1-in-3 ramp's ends and its sides along a landing at its own height stay walkable.
  - The sub-steps stop a fast pounce, or a long frame, from tunnelling over a rim.
  - The spec says "the Gorge's cliff walls are colliders". This plan makes a rim a wall by height alone, so a cliff face needs no circle colliders to stop the cat. Plan 2 may still add colliders where a push reads better. The camera meets cliff feet through R8 either way.
  - Cost if wrong: a rim plan 2 wants walkable comes out blocked, or the reverse. That is a constant change and a fixture test.
- **R3. Sliding along an edge.**
  - `ground.slide(px, pz, pos)` returns `false` and leaves `pos` alone when the step from `(px,pz)` is legal.
  - Otherwise it keeps the x half (`pos.z = pz`) if that is legal, else the z half (`pos.x = px`), else both. It returns `true`.
  - It runs last in the player's movement, after the collider push, the water pass and the bounds clamp, so nothing can push the cat over a rim after it.
  - Cost if wrong: if a collider sits on a rim, a cat can stop dead there. Plan 2 keeps colliders off rims (Review Focus 1).
- **R4. `player.perchY` stays relative: metres above the ground under the cat.**
  - The cat's world y is `ground.y(cat) + perchY + hopY (+ fightLift)`.
  - The four `perchY === 0` tests (player.js 636 and 726, main.js 917, rivalcats.js 192) keep their meaning and are not edited. avatar.js 74 (`paws … setY(player.perchY)`) and 78 (`perchY = 0` on release) do change: 74 adds the ground (Task 4), and 78 already means "back on the ground".
  - Every authored height in data becomes "metres above the ground at that object's own x, z", and no number changes. That covers `perch.y`, `GOLD_MICE[].y`, knockable `y` and `fallTo.y`, collectible `y`, rest `floor`, `keepSlots` `at.y`, camp leader rock `h`, and `sureClawsTreePerch`'s trunk-top rule.
  - Cost if wrong: every perch comparison has to be redone in world space. The climbing rule (R5) already compares in world space, so that saving is the whole point of this choice.
- **R5. Climbing reads heights in world space, and drops stay on the cat's own level.**
  - `bestPerch(..., opts)` gains `opts.ground` (default `FLAT_GROUND`). A candidate perch whose ground differs from the ground under the cat by more than `LEVEL_TOL = 0.5` m is skipped, whichever way the difference goes.
  - The height test becomes `(perch.y + dy) - currentY`, where `dy = ground.y(perch) - ground.y(cat)`. The reach test (`perch.y > 1 ? reachHigh : reachLow`) keeps reading the prop's own relative height.
  - `canFenceRun` gets the same `dy`.
  - The "prefer the highest" pick (climbing.js:392, `pp.y > best.y`) compares world heights (`pp.y + dy`) when `!ground.flat`.
  - Hop-down (`player.perchY = 0`) lands on the ground under the perch, which is the ground right below the cat. So the spec's "drops are limited to perch-sized hops onto the ground right below" holds with no new code at the drop.
  - Cost if wrong: a Gorge perch that straddles two levels gets skipped. Plan 2 places perches on one level.
- **R6. Uphill pace.**
  - The target speed is multiplied by `1 − UPHILL_SLOW · clamp(grade / MAX_PATH_GRADE, 0, 1)`, with `UPHILL_SLOW = 0.15` and `MAX_PATH_GRADE = 1/3`.
  - `grade` is `ground.gradient(p) · dir`, the slope of the shape under the cat along the input direction. A finite difference would read a cliff foot as a steep climb and slow the cat walking toward any wall.
  - Downhill and level ground are unchanged. It composes with the water pace and the scare freeze the way `waterPace` does.
  - Cost if wrong: one constant.
- **R7. Slope tilt.**
  - Every cat that walks (the player's cat, remote co-walkers, strays, clan cats, intruders, ghosts, the kitten) pitches and rolls to the ground's slope under it, so the paws sit on the slope plane rather than floating or sinking. The spec lists "floating above a slope" as an anti-goal.
  - `groundTilt(ground, x, z, yaw, out)` returns `{ pitch, roll }` from the slope's gradient, each capped at `TILT_MAX = 0.35` rad. It uses the `'YXZ'` order convention that the fight build introduced (`applyWaterLook`'s `fightTilt`).
  - The cat's and remote cats' tilt is eased at `TILT_RATE = 10`/s and passed into `applyWaterLook`, which after the fight merge is "the one place the game writes a walking cat group's rotation.x and rotation.z". A fight's tilt replaces it; fights stand on level ground.
  - NPC cats set their tilt directly with no easing.
  - On `ground.flat` nothing is passed and no rotation is written that is not written today.
  - Cost if wrong: if the owner wants "slightly", the tilt becomes a fraction of the slope, which is a constant. Paws then sink a little uphill.
- **R8. Camera rock and ceiling avoidance**, only when `!ground.flat`.
  - The camera is placed on the segment from the cat's look point `L = avatar + (0, CAT_LOOK_Y, 0)` to the wanted camera position `W = avatar + camOff`, after the existing pull: `camera = L + f·(W − L)`.
  - `cameraRockFraction(L, W, ground) → f` marches 12 samples from L toward W and finds the first bad one. A sample is bad when it is below `ground.y + CAM_ROCK_CLEAR (0.35)` or above `ground.ceil − CAM_CEIL_CLEAR (0.3)`. It then bisects 5 times between the last good and the first bad sample, so `f` moves continuously as the cat walks rather than in twelfths.
  - Its floor is `MIN_CAM_FRAC`, set so that `|f·(W − L)| ≥ MIN_CAM_DIST = 0.3` m. The camera may come right in behind the head at a cliff foot. It is never raised by a jump.
  - The fraction snaps in on the same frame (a camera that lags is a camera seen through rock) and eases back out with the existing `PULL_HOLD` / `PULL_OUT_SPEED` rule.
  - After that, `MIN_CAMERA_Y` applies above `ground.y` under the camera, and the camera is held at or below `ceil − CAM_CEIL_CLEAR`. `validateGround` requires every ceiling to be at least `MIN_CAMERA_Y + CAM_CEIL_CLEAR = 0.8` m above the ground under it, so these two clamps can never cross.
  - Cave framing (in close and low) and any nicer cliff-foot framing are tuned in plan 3. This is the engine.
  - Cost if wrong: at a cliff foot the camera sits close behind the cat's head until she turns, which looks crowded. Plan 3 can add a pitch-up there. Review Focus 3.
- **R9. NPC walkers respect edges.**
  - Strays, clan cats, ghosts and the kitten move freely. Each keeps its previous x/z before its per-frame step and calls `ground.slide` at its existing x/z chokepoint (straycats.js `keepOut`, ghosts.js `keepOutPoint`, the clancats roamer/buddy/homeward writes, kitten.js's two writes). It then sets `y = ground.y(x, z)`.
  - Critters move freely when they flee or run to a hide (critters.js 480, 488-489 and 527: `p.addScaledVector`), so those writes slide too. Hides (`Vector3(h.x, 0, h.z)`, critters.js:197) are seated at `ground.y`, so `distanceTo(hideAt) <= HIDE_REACH` (518) still succeeds off the base.
  - Intruders, rival patrols and the yard dog follow authored anchors or routes. They only read `ground.y`, and plan 2 or plan 4 validates their routes against the ground.
  - Scripted moves of the player's cat also respect rims:
    - The rival chase's scamper (rivalcats.js 293-317) lands on a spot chosen by `findLanding` with `isWalkableAt`. With `ground`, a landing is rejected unless `ground.canStep(from, landing)`.
    - The fight's walk-over adds `canStep` from the cat's current x/z to its per-step test (R11).
  - Cost if wrong: a route-following actor crosses a rim on a Gorge route. Plan 2 and plan 4 tests catch it.
- **R10. Proximity across levels.** 3D `distanceTo` checks already exclude a cat 3 m above. The 2D `Math.hypot` checks gain one shared gate, `ground.sameLevel(ax, az, bx, bz)`, true when `|y(a) − y(b)| ≤ LEVEL_TOL`. `FLAT_GROUND.sameLevel` is always true, so on flat maps nothing changes. It applies to:
  - encounters: the intruder challenge (intruders.js 426/436), the rival chase trigger (rivalcats.js), the kitten (kitten.js:137) and the clan fight's start;
  - the prompt checks in game/interactions.js: scenic spots (493, 1138), scent and digs (609, 631), the quest target (796).
  - The perched-collectible check (interactions.js:751) compares world heights: `ground.y + perch.y` against `ground.y + c.y`.
  - Cost if wrong: on the clifftop she digs up a find on the floor below, or an intruder on the floor challenges her. Each is a one-line gate.
- **R11. Fights stand on one level.**
  - `fightReach`'s `ok` and the per-frame staging check also reject a spot whose ground differs from my anchor spot's by more than `FIGHT_LEVEL_TOL = 0.3`.
  - The cat's y comes from the avatar's base y (R4, Task 3), which includes the ground. `s.fightLift` stays relative.
  - `intruders.stage(visit, { y })` takes a **relative** lift. clanfight passes `itLift` unchanged, and `intruders.place()` adds `ground.y(st.x, st.z)`, which is Task 6's job.
  - The dust bursts become `ground.y(mid) + 0.2`.
  - The walk-over's per-step test adds `canStep` (R9).
  - The option is named `terrain`, not `ground`: the fight build's `createClanFight` already has a local `const ground = (x, z) => isGround(x, z) === true`.
  - The fight camera's ring heights, `LOOK_Y` and `CAT_HEIGHTS` ride `ground.y(mid)`, and its candidates are rejected by the same rock and ceiling test as R8.
  - Cost if wrong: plan 4's "fights only on the floor and wide ledges" keeps fights there anyway.
- **R12. The yarn ball** uses `ground.y + RADIUS` as its floor and bounces off rims. If a step is illegal, x/z revert and the horizontal velocity reverses at half speed. It never rolls off a clifftop.
  - Cost if wrong: a ball lost down the gorge, which can be fetched by walking the path. Low.
- **R13. No wire change.**
  - Remote cats, the remote toy ghost, remote meow and toy events, and the snipe toast's distance (netevents.js 180, 186) all take y from the receiver's own `ground.y` at the streamed `[x, z]`.
  - Remote cats that are perched still draw at ground level, as they do today on every map.
  - Cost if wrong: if the owner wants perched co-walkers drawn on their perch, that is a `pos` length-3 wire change with a validator bump. Out of scope.
- **R14. The den, the sky and the water keep their absolute heights.**
  - The den is never given height: `dencats.js` and `denlive.js` stay as they are, with a comment.
  - Skylife birds, the UFO, clouds and the rainbow are sky.
  - Water meshes keep their authored `y`. Water lies only where `ground.y === base`, and plan 2's map test checks every water footprint against that. This keeps `render/splash.js`'s absolute drop heights (225, 229) and the water pass right. `WATERLINE` and the sink stay relative to the base y that `applyWaterLook` is given, which Task 3 makes include the ground.
  - Some particles are drawn round the camera or the cat, and these ride the ground instead (Task 7):
    - `render/ambient.js`: leaves come to rest at `ground.y + LEAF_REST_Y` (488), and the spray's `by` (509) is lifted by the ground.
    - `weather.js`: the rain column's `position.y` is set to `ground.y` under the camera.
- **R15. Static props sit on the ground through a helper.**
  - `seatOnGround(object3d, ground, { children = false } = {})` adds `ground.y(position.x, position.z)` to a placed prop's `position.y`. With `children: true` it seats each direct child of a group at the origin instead; `addCamp` (world/camp.js:103-140) builds such groups. Plan 2's world file uses it after each builder factory call, so no builder signature changes.
  - Props placed per walk (border posts, gifts, tippables, rests, the gnome and unicorn, gates, the scratch post, the race pad) read `ground.y` directly.

## Review Focus

1. **A pounce or zoomies straight at a rim on a slow phone** (a frame of 0.1 s, 9 m/s, so 0.9 m in one step): the cat stops at the rim and never lands on the floor below. Tested in Task 3 (`'a pounce at a rim lands short even on a 0.1 s frame'`).
2. **A perch on a plateau near its rim, and a perch on the floor below within reach:** pressing climb on the plateau never picks the floor perch, and hopping down lands on the plateau. Tested in Task 4 (`'a drop never leaves the cat’s level'`).
3. **Backing the cat into a cliff foot at the furthest zoom, then walking away:** the camera never goes below the rock above it, never jumps, and does not pump in and out at the rim. Tested in Task 5 (`'the camera is never inside rock and never pumps at a cliff foot'`).
4. **A stray whose wander target is across a rim:** it stops at the rim and never snaps 3 m up or down. Tested in Task 6 (`'a stray never crosses a rim'`).
5. **The yarn ball thrown toward a clifftop edge:** it stays on the clifftop. Tested in Task 7 (`'the yarn ball never rolls off a rim'`).
